# Why Computer Vision is the New Business Intelligence

> From grayscale pixels to actionable business metrics — how CNNs work, what the Tesla vs. Waymo pure vision debate reveals about the technology's limits, and how B2B companies apply computer vision to unlock new intelligence.

## Overview

Machine vision is not just a robotics technology. B2B companies are using CNNs to measure shelf compliance, count pedestrian flow, detect equipment anomalies, and quantify sentiment from facial expressions in video. This article explains how the technology works — from grayscale encoding through convolution and pooling to classification — and frames the Tesla vs. Waymo "pure vision vs. LiDAR" debate as a lens for understanding the real capabilities and constraints of vision-only systems.

## Key Topics / Sections

- **The Death of Geometry**: Why traditional computer vision (edges, keypoints, geometry) gave way to learned feature extraction — and why this shift was necessary for real-world performance at scale.
- **The "Pure Vision" Core**: Tesla's argument for cameras-only autonomous driving; what pure vision can achieve at scale and where it struggles (adverse weather, edge cases without training data).
- **Scaling Down: Max Pooling**: How max pooling reduces spatial dimensionality while preserving the strongest feature activations — making CNNs computationally tractable.
- **The Learning Loop**: How CNNs train on labelled image datasets using backpropagation — the weight update process that teaches the network to recognise features automatically.
- **The Payoff: Tracking & Segmentation**: Business applications of CNNs beyond classification — object tracking, instance segmentation, and the metrics these unlock for retail, logistics, and facilities management.

## Key Takeaways

- CNN feature learning removes the need for hand-engineered feature detectors, but requires large labelled datasets
- Pure vision systems work remarkably well at scale — the challenge is the long tail of rare scenarios with insufficient training data
- B2B computer vision value comes from converting visual data into structured metrics that replace manual observation
