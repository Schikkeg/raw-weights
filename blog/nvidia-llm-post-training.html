SFT Bootstrap — The Math Teacher Analogy

A teacher who jumps straight to calculus with a student who has never seen addition will get nowhere — the student is never correct, so there's nothing to praise or build on. RL has the same problem: if the model never produces a correct answer, the reward is always zero and learning stalls. SFT is the addition warmup. Move the slider, then press Train.

← No SFT (pure RL from scratch)    Heavy SFT warmup →

Red: RL from scratch — model never gets signal, stays flat
Green: With SFT warmup — RL amplifies from the starting point

Key: SFT only needs to get you to "sometimes correct" — RL does the heavy lifting from there.

RLVR — Verifiable vs Subjective Rewards

Ask five people to rate a poem and you get five different scores — the training signal is noisy, inconsistent, unreliable. Ask a computer whether tic-tac-toe was won — you get a clean binary 1 or 0. RLVR means only training on tasks where a computer can verify correctness. Press Sample Attempt → to run examples and watch the quality signals diverge.

Reward Hacking — Cheese vs Banana

A mouse is rewarded for reaching the cheese. But there's a banana on the path — also yellow, also rewarding, much closer. In a single fixed environment, the mouse learns to go to the banana every time and stops there. Change the environment layout each time (as 70+ simultaneous envs do) and the banana shortcut stops working — the mouse must find the actual goal. Try both modes below.

Multi-Environment RL — The School Exam Analogy

A student who only studies one subject can cram it and ace that exam — but learns nothing transferable. Examine across 12 subjects simultaneously and cramming one becomes useless. Toggle environments on or off below, then press Train → to see what happens.

GRPO — Group Relative Policy Optimization

A writing teacher asks 8 students to draft an answer. She keeps only the above-average ones and has everyone learn from those. Next round, the average is higher — so the bar rises again. GRPO does exactly this with LLM outputs. Work through all 3 rounds to watch the floor rise.

RLHF vs RLVR — Order Changes Everything

RLVR makes the model capable — it can actually solve hard tasks. RLHF makes the model pleasant — humans prefer its tone, it refuses gracefully. Both matter. But if you do RLHF first, you train the model to give polished refusals before it learns to actually help. Then RLVR has to fight that already-baked preference. Press Run Both Orders → to see the difference.
