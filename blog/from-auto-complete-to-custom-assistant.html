From Auto-Complete to Custom Assistant: A Visual Guide to LLM Post-Training

Custom LLMs

Apr 13, 2026

It’s easy to forget that if you strip the "assistant" persona away from ChatGPT or Gemini, what you are fundamentally left with is just a very powerful auto-complete.

During the pre-training phase, models ingest trillions of tokens just to learn how to predict the next word. But how do we turn that raw, probabilistic text engine into a helpful, instruction-following assistant? More importantly, how do we make it deeply understand our specific enterprise data and business context?

"Many teams start with RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation). But when RAG isn't enough—when you need to fundamentally change the model's tone, format, or embed deep, nuanced domain knowledge—you need to build a custom LLM."

Inspired by an incredible lecture by Maxime Labonne (Liquid AI) at MIT, I realized that the path to creating custom models—the Post-Training Pipeline—is often gated behind dense academic papers and walls of text. I wanted to change that.

Why I Built a Visual-First Tool

Concepts like Process Reward Models, DARE-TIES Merging, and Direct Preference Optimization are deeply mathematical. Reading about them is exhausting. I built this interactive guide completely around custom-designed infographics and step-by-step diagrams. Instead of reading about tree-search logic, you can visually trace how an LLM scores individual steps and loops them back to form a perfect answer. Visuals map abstract math directly to human intuition.

Inside the Interactive Guide

I packaged this entire journey into a interactive tool: The Custom LLM Post-Training Guide. Here is a breakdown of the interactive modules you can explore inside:

1. Data Preparation

The most critical step. Learn how to synthesize data without causing "diversity collapse," and balance the three pillars: Accuracy, Diversity, and Complexity.

2. Training Algorithms

Visual breakdowns of how Full Fine-Tuning compares to parameter-efficient methods like LoRA, and why DPO is overtaking PPO for preference alignment.

3. Model Merging

Why train from scratch? Explore the visual math behind SLERP and DARE-TIES, showing how to fuse a Coding AI with a Language AI to create a super-model.

4. The Evaluation Paradox

Automated benchmarks (like MMLU) correlate poorly with human preference [16]. See how to combine Human Rating, LLM Judges, and automated scripts.

5. Test-Time Compute (The New Frontier)

Scaling compute isn't just for training anymore. Our diagrams break down how Process Reward Models (PRMs) score reasoning step-by-step to outperform massive frontier models using smaller architectures.
