# The Reality of Agentic Engineering: AI Dev 26 Takeaways

> A personal recap from AI Dev 26 — what I tried, who I talked to, the hard questions I posed to speakers, and what the conference revealed about where agentic engineering actually stands today.

## Overview

AI Dev 26 brought together 25,000 tools, dozens of speakers, and an honest engineering community wrestling with the gap between agentic AI hype and production reality. This recap covers the highlights, the networking moments, and the post-session deep dives where the real substance lives — including candid discussions with speakers on federated learning complexity, multi-agent sandboxing, and UX challenges for autonomous systems.

## Key Topics / Sections

- **What I Loved & Tried**: Hands-on highlights including Google DeepMind's Paige Bailey on Gemini audio and video models; Cursor's Amrita Venkatraman on cloud-native agent factories; live tool demos.
- **Post-Session Deep Dives**: Hard questions posed to speakers after their sessions:
  - Federated learning complexity with Daniel Beutel (Flower Labs) — how to ensure robustness with uneven client data distributions
  - Multi-agent sandboxing — what isolation primitives actually exist when agents call agents
  - Privacy implications when agents hold session state across user interactions
  - UX patterns for surfaces where the agent's reasoning is not visible to the user
- **Networking Highlights**: Meeting fellow builders, cohort members, and the candid hallway conversations that don't make the livestream.
- **Key Debate**: The fundamental tension between capability (what agents can do) and controllability (what they should do) — and whether the field is investing in the right half.

## Key Takeaways

- The 25,000-tool landscape exposes the need for tool governance — not just tool availability
- Sandboxing between agents in a multi-agent system is an unsolved problem in production
- UX for agentic systems requires new patterns; existing chat UX does not map to autonomous execution
