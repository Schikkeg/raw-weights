Deep Generative Modeling

Explore the foundations of AI capable of creating brand new data.

Part 1

The Big Picture

🎨 Generative Modeling (The "Creator")

Instead of just looking at pictures and learning to slap a label on them, this AI studies thousands of examples to learn the actual "recipe" of the data. Once it knows the recipe, it can generate brand-new, realistic examples that have never existed before.

🕵️ Unsupervised Learning

Learning without an Answer Key! The model does not use human-provided labels to learn. It figures out the hidden rules and structures entirely on its own just by observing the raw data.

The Core Capabilities

  • Density Estimation: Figures out the "hidden ingredients" to understand the overall shape and spread of the data.
  • Sample Generation: Using the learned "recipe" to actually create brand-new, realistic data instances.
  • Outlier Detection: Spotting highly unusual events (like a hazard for a self-driving car) because it knows what "normal" looks like.
  • Data Debiasing: Tells if certain features are under-represented, allowing humans to fix data and build fairer systems.

Part 4

The Foundation: Autoencoders

🦇

Latent Variables (Plato's Cave)

Imagine you are in a cave and can only see shadows moving on the wall. The shadows are the data we observe (like pixels in an image), but the latent variables are the actual, hidden 3D objects casting those shadows.

Method 1: Autoencoders (The Summarizer)

The architecture of compressing data into a hidden representation and forcing the network to redraw it perfectly.

Part 5

Method 2: Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)

The Flexible Summarizer: A mathematical evolution designed to generate brand new data.

🎲

Stochasticity (Adding "Wiggle Room")

Instead of a single, rigid summary, it learns a flexible range defined by a mean and a standard deviation.

🗂️

Regularization (The Organizer)

Forces the latent space to have Continuity and Completeness (KL Divergence).

🧮

The Reparameterization Trick

Isolates randomness into a "dice roll" constant (epsilon), keeping calculations smooth for backpropagation.

Method 3: GANs & Cycle GANs

🎭

GANs

The Forger vs. The Detective

  • The Generator

    Starts with completely random noise and tries to paint a realistic fake picture.

  • The Discriminator

    Looks at both fake and real pictures, and tries to classify which is which.

🦓

Cycle GANs

The Unpaired Translators

Two pairs of generators/discriminators working together to translate data directly from one style to another (e.g. horses to zebras).

Evolution of Generative Modeling

📜
Pre-1980s

Foundational Statistics

Latent Variables, Density Estimation, and Outlier Detection. Foundational statistical concepts that have existed for decades.

🗜️
1986

Autoencoders

The concept of using neural networks to mathematically compress and reconstruct data unsupervised, popularized by Geoffrey Hinton.

🎲
2013

Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)

Introduced by Kingma and Welling, bringing the crucial "probabilistic twist" that allowed models to smoothly generate brand new data.

⚔️
2014

Generative Adversarial Networks

The "forger vs. detective" framework famously invented by Ian Goodfellow, kicking off a massive boom in high-quality image generation.

🔄
2017

Cycle GANs

Architecture using pairs of generators and discriminators for unpaired style translation, introduced by Jun-Yan Zhu at UC Berkeley.

💡 Frequently Asked Questions (Generative Modeling)

What is the main difference between supervised and unsupervised learning?▼
Supervised learning relies on a dataset where data comes with explicit human-provided labels. Unsupervised learning uses raw data without any labels. Generative modeling is unsupervised.
How does the "Plato's Cave" analogy explain latent variables?▼
Prisoners can only see shadows moving on a wall (the observable data we see, like pixels). The true 3D objects casting shadows represent the latent variables, the hidden features governing the data distribution.
Why do Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) use the Reparameterization Trick?▼
VAEs introduce randomness. This pure randomness breaks the predictable math required for backpropagation. The trick solves this by isolating randomness into a separate constant called epsilon.
How do Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) learn to create realistic data?▼
GANs employ a Generator (the "forger") and a Discriminator (the "detective"). The competing objective forces the generator to continuously improve its fakes until it can completely fool the discriminator.
